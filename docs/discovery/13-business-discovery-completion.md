# I01 Business Discovery: Executive Closeout

**Fictional simulated portfolio exercise.** Northstar Digital Group and all testimony are invented. The objective was to discover a defensible business problem, actors, journeys, constraints, and evidence gaps for AtlasGate, an enterprise API platform with control-plane and data-plane concerns, before approving requirements or architecture.

Eight [sessions](sessions/README.md) were recorded: executive, product, security/compliance, reliability/operations, Commerce/Loyalty domain, external consumer, architecture alignment, and synthesis. SES-007 was executed before SES-006; the later direct consumer account refined architecture questions. [SES-008](sessions/SES-008-discovery-synthesis-and-baseline-review/02-session-record.md) accepted the [Business Discovery Baseline](baseline/README.md) and closed I01.

The evidence sufficiently frames plausible coordination and completion friction; distinct producer, consumer, reviewer, platform, and support roles; contrasting Commerce, Loyalty, and external journeys; domain semantic ownership; cross-boundary handoffs; and the need to distinguish provider activity from consumer outcome. Stable outcome themes are predictability, usable adoption, accountable governance, safer change, and operability. [Confidence](baseline/evidence-confidence.md) is moderate at best for recurring themes and low or insufficient for prevalence, legal scope, measurement, cost, and solution fitness.

The [requirement inputs](baseline/requirement-inputs.md) cover delivery, external usability, change/adoption, access and evidence, reliability measurement, support, and value. The [architecture inputs](baseline/architecture-inputs.md) carry authority, management/runtime failure, policy, metadata, publication, operational context, and control-plane option questions. They are analysis inputs, not approved requirements or decisions.

[Unresolved items](baseline/unresolved-items.md) retain owner-assigned work on representative journeys and consumers, obligations and formal authority, outcome and telemetry definitions, domain variation, incident evidence, cost, and option failure behavior. Major residual risks are building for unmeasured delay, treating one fictional partner as representative, confusing coordination with authority, inferring service targets without measurement, and selecting a structure before failure and operating-cost comparison. These were accepted for discovery exit only.

No Requirements Baseline, Architecture Baseline, ADR, compliance conclusion, service commitment, technology selection, migration mandate, or implementation is approved. The next decision point is a future Requirements Baseline review after source-backed validation and conflict resolution; architecture options can be assessed in parallel for a later separate Architecture Baseline decision. This closeout claims disciplined simulated discovery and traceability, not real organizational findings or implemented capability.
