# SES-008 - Discovery Synthesis and Baseline Review: Simulated Transcript

This is a fictional simulated portfolio meeting. Northstar Digital Group, all participants, examples, and evidence are fictional; no real meeting occurred. Daniel facilitates Maya, Priya, Elena, Marcus, Aisha, Lucas, Jordan, and Sofia. Rachel Kim's distinct SES-006 consumer account is considered through its record. This dialogue makes discovery-phase judgments only; analysis follows in the [session record](02-session-record.md).

## 1. Problem, outcomes, and representation

1. **Daniel Okafor:** What business problems are sufficiently established to proceed, and where do we only have plausible claims?
2. **Maya Chen:** Unpredictable coordination across API delivery and consumption is a credible investment problem. I will not repeat the early “months” estimate as a measured baseline or assign all delay to the platform.
3. **Aisha Bello:** Commerce launch examples support handoff friction, but partner readiness caused some waiting. A general platform delay claim would erase that distinction.
4. **Daniel:** Which desired outcomes are stable enough to guide analysis, without setting targets?
5. **Maya:** Predictable completion, usable adoption, accountable governance, safer change, and operable outcomes are stable themes. Savings and improvement magnitude remain unproved.
6. **Jordan Lee:** Counting APIs or logins would hide whether a developer can complete an intended call. We need completion evidence and eligible journey definitions.
7. **Daniel:** Which stakeholder and consumer groups must remain represented?
8. **Sofia Novak:** Producers, internal consumers, partner developers, platform operators, Security, Compliance, domains, and support all matter. Rachel's partner view added a different completion clock; one simulated external consumer does not establish prevalence.
9. **Lucas Pereira:** Loyalty consumers still have no direct interview. Do not treat my producer account as their answer.

## 2. Journeys, recurrence, and challenged assumptions

10. **Daniel:** Which current journeys are sufficiently understood as analysis inputs, and where are material gaps?
11. **Aisha:** Commerce partner launch and tenant change have distinct approval and partner dependencies. Their stages are useful maps, but no ticket or elapsed-time sample validates frequency or cause.
12. **Lucas:** Loyalty semantic change and consumer adoption are also distinct from deployment. We lack a consumer inventory and closure record.
13. **Sofia:** External onboarding, first usable access, change, and incident communication are separate partner tasks. Rachel's account tests internal assumptions, but we have no observed document task or agreement.
14. **Daniel:** Which findings recur across independent sessions, and which depend on one perspective?
15. **Priya Raman:** Handoff context, distinct domain meaning, producer versus consumer completion, and cross-boundary ownership recur across product, domain, operations, and the external account. Management outage behavior, alternative cost, and traffic shape are still mainly reasoning or one account.
16. **Elena García:** Repetition among our internal participants is not independent source verification. Review variability and exception closure remain questions until records and authority are inspected.
17. **Daniel:** Which earlier assumptions were challenged or limited?
18. **Aisha:** One universal workflow and security as the principal delay were challenged. Shared concerns can justify analysis without identical sequences.
19. **Jordan:** A central page, issued credential, sent notice, or producer deployment cannot serve as a completion proxy. Rachel's case makes that limitation concrete, though it cannot quantify prevalence.
20. **Daniel:** Which domain variations look necessary, and which look like accidental inconsistency?
21. **Lucas:** Consumer release timing and Loyalty semantics are candidates for necessary variation. Copied configuration and missing owner context look like accidental inconsistency candidates; neither classification is proven.

## 3. Requirements, uncertainty, and operating boundaries

22. **Daniel:** What must be resolved before requirements approval, and what can wait until architecture or implementation planning?
23. **Elena:** Applicable policy, law, contract, classification, exception authority, and evidence purpose need authoritative review before security or compliance requirements are approved. No compliance applicability is legally confirmed.
24. **Marcus Reed:** Critical journey boundaries, usable signals, event definitions, and consumer consequences precede any reliability target. No SLO, SLA, error budget, quota, or numeric service target can be inferred from these sessions.
25. **Aisha:** Could we make a mandatory shared publication path a requirement now? Teams keep describing missing context.
26. **Daniel:** No. That turns a problem into a required mechanism before return causes, domain variation, and adoption cost are tested. It stays a requirement-analysis input.
27. **Sofia:** Requirements analysis must include external usability, authorized first call, partner-controlled waiting, change comprehension, release capacity, accepted support routing, and consumer-visible recovery. Rachel's preferences are not contractual commitments.
28. **Jordan:** Ownership is also not solved by routing a ticket to Platform Support. We can coordinate and supply context without taking domain semantics or business risk acceptance.
29. **Maya:** Cost and capacity sources can mature during option work, but formal adoption and exception decision rights cannot remain vague when requirements are approved.

## 4. Architecture drivers and alternatives

30. **Daniel:** Which architecture drivers have enough evidence to carry forward, which alternatives remain open, and which future ADR topics need only more evidence?
31. **Priya:** Domain semantic authority, management versus request-time failure behavior, policy responsibility, publication traceability, and operational consequence are decision inputs. Centralized, modular, and distributed control-plane alternatives remain open; current project direction is a hypothesis for assessment.
32. **Jordan:** I would choose local runtime enforcement now to avoid a management outage taking traffic down.
33. **Marcus:** I object to choosing it from an outage story. Safe stale-state behavior, configuration activation, and detection have not been tested; local enforcement can fail differently.
34. **Priya:** Agreed. We reject that architecture choice. Control/runtime separation, module boundaries, policy placement, change propagation, revision history, and metadata authority are future ADR topics for continued evidence gathering, not drafted decisions.
35. **Elena:** Also reject “retain everything” as the answer to traceability. Evidence content and retention depend on purpose and authoritative obligations.
36. **Aisha:** A modular control plane could still create a central approval queue. Compare real launch waiting and rollback before preferring it.

38. **Maya:** The next work is requirements analysis, not an implementation plan, mandated migration, technology selection, contract promise, or numeric target. I accept that simulated testimony has no source-backed prevalence, with owners assigned to validate before later baselines.

40. **Maya:** Yes. Accept the reviewed Business Discovery Baseline: this session's record, the baseline index and five baseline records, plus the linked SES-001 through SES-007 evidence and live uncertainty and risk registers.
