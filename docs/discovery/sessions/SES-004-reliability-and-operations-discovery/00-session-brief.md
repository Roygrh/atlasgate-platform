# SES-004 - Reliability and Operations Discovery Brief

> **Fictional simulation:** Northstar Digital Group, AtlasGate, every participant, event, estimate, and record in this activity are fictional portfolio material. No real organization or production operation is represented.

## Purpose and participants

Daniel Okafor facilitates and protects discovery scope. Marcus Reed brings SRE, telemetry, and incident-analysis questions. Jordan Lee represents shared-platform operation and support. Aisha Bello represents commerce outcomes and domain ownership. Lucas Pereira represents loyalty producers and internal consumers. Sofia Novak represents external partners and their support boundary. Priya Raman tests cross-boundary and architecture assumptions without selecting a design.

## Objectives

- Identify candidate critical journeys and how criticality varies by consumer, consequence, and period.
- Explore detection, diagnosis, coordination, communication, mitigation, restoration, closure, and risk-acceptance responsibilities.
- Inventory possible incident/support records and telemetry without claiming availability.
- Frame candidate SLIs separately from targets, SLOs, SLAs, and approved requirements.
- Finish with evidence requests, limitations, and fictional owners rather than consensus.

## Agenda

1. Evidence rules and meaning of criticality.
2. Commerce launch degradation.
3. Loyalty change incident.
4. Cross-boundary ownership incident.
5. Production support and telemetry gap.
6. Candidate telemetry, indicators, target-setting cautions, and evidence commitments.

## Preparation artifacts

Use SES-001 through SES-003 statements, findings, journeys, security context, evidence requirements, risks, assumptions, and open questions only as unvalidated context. Requested source material includes sanitized incident timelines, support contacts, ownership sources, change records, partner reports, telemetry inventories, access records, and retention/access rules.

## Planned questions

- Which outcome fails, for whom, and during which period?
- What detects the issue, what diagnoses it, and what proves restoration?
- Where do domain, platform, consumer, partner, coordinator, communicator, and risk-acceptance responsibilities change?
- Which signals may exist, who produces and consumes them, and what coverage, integrity, retention, or access limits apply?
- How should Northstar-controlled time and external waiting be separated?
- What evidence is required before any numeric target or commitment is proposed?

## Required scenarios

The simulation keeps four scenarios distinct: intermittent commerce-partner failures or latency during a dated launch; incorrect or unavailable loyalty behavior after a change; uncertain domain/platform/partner/access ownership; and diagnosis constrained by incomplete telemetry, ownership metadata, or safe support access.

## Evidence and sensitive-information rules

Claims and recalled estimates remain attributed and explicitly unverified. Dialogue contains no analytical IDs. Derived records must link back to transcript sections and preserve conflicts. Operational telemetry is not automatically durable decision or audit evidence. No secrets, payloads, personal data, credentials, or sensitive operational detail may be copied; only sanitized metadata and purpose-limited samples may be requested. Safe support-access questions inherit SES-003 boundaries and do not weaken authorization, attribution, minimization, removal, or closure expectations.

## Non-goals

This session will not approve requirements, criticality tiers, ownership models, incident severities, support commitments, SLIs, SLOs, SLAs, error budgets, availability/latency/recovery targets, architecture, products, tools, alerts, dashboards, telemetry retention, or executable behavior.

## Exit criteria

All seven perspectives and four scenarios are recorded; disagreements, incomplete evidence, unverified estimates, workarounds, false/missing signals, and evidence limitations are explicit; candidate indicators remain target-free; and follow-ups have fictional owners.
