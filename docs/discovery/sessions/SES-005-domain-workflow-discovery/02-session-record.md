# SES-005 - Domain Workflow Discovery: Session Record

> **Fictional simulation:** This record synthesizes a fictional simulated session. It does not describe real people or operations and does not validate the statements or analysis.

## Summary and participants

Daniel Okafor facilitated Aisha Bello, Lucas Pereira, Jordan Lee, Priya Raman, and Marcus Reed. The comparison found shared governance and information concerns but no evidence for an identical Commerce and Loyalty sequence. Domain teams retain semantic authority; coordination by Platform Engineering does not itself transfer authority; producer deployment and consumer adoption are different events.

## Scenario results

| Scenario | Preliminary result | Main ambiguity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commerce partner launch | External date, partner readiness, review, release, and production consequence interact. | Completion and delay attribution span parties. |
| Commerce tenant-specific policy change | Urgency and different treatment raise business-meaning, exception, and recovery questions. | Necessary variation versus copied inconsistency. |
| Loyalty semantic API change | Internal consumers interpret domain meaning and adopt asynchronously. | Consumer inventory, compatibility, and downstream interpretation. |
| Loyalty migration/version transition | Multiple schedules can leave coexistence and closure unresolved after deployment. | Completion authority and residual-use acceptance. |

## Common and domain-specific concerns

Common candidates are change context, discoverability, impact evidence, ownership visibility, review predictability, dependency coordination, support boundaries, compatibility reasoning, and explicit completion criteria. Commerce variation includes external dates, partner readiness, commercial treatment, and potentially concentrated launch criticality. Loyalty variation includes semantic interpretation, indirect internal consumers, independent schedules, and migration closure. None is validated enterprise-wide.

## Tensions

- Shared evidence consistency versus a universal workflow.
- Platform coordination versus domain semantic authority.
- Producer responsibility versus consumer adoption responsibility.
- Deployment completion versus end-to-end change completion.
- Urgent business action versus review, exception, and recovery evidence.
- Necessary domain variation versus avoidable inconsistent context and handoffs.
- Locally fast direct chat or copied treatment versus hidden support, drift, and cleanup cost.

## Discovery-process decisions

Only method decisions were made: maintain four distinct scenario records; classify common concerns separately from sequence; represent coordination separately from authority transfer; record estimates as unverified; and require source-backed samples before proposing requirements.

## Explicit non-decisions

No target or universal workflow, requirement, RACI, ownership model, domain boundary, completion definition, policy mechanism, architecture, technology, vendor, control, service target, migration approach, or implementation was approved. External Consumer Discovery, Architecture Alignment, and Discovery Synthesis remain planned and incomplete.
