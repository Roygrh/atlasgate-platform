# SES-006 - External Consumer Discovery: Session Record

Fictional, simulated portfolio evidence only. Rachel Kim and Meridian Retail Partners are fictional. This record analyzes the [36-exchange transcript](01-simulated-transcript.md); it is not a real partner interview, contract, approval, or representative sample.

## External-consumer observations

Rachel distinguishes [API discoverability from use-case fit](01-simulated-transcript.md#1-first-time-partner-onboarding), available documents from usable examples and semantics, issued credentials from successful authorized calls, and producer deployment from consumer readiness. In the [access case](01-simulated-transcript.md#2-credential-and-access-problem), unclear ownership and repeated handoffs increase waiting; safe symptom evidence should exclude credentials and broad payloads. In the [change case](01-simulated-transcript.md#3-breaking-or-semantic-change), notice, semantic impact, partner release capacity, verification, and residual-use closure are separate. In the [incident case](01-simulated-transcript.md#4-traffic-and-support-incident), traffic expectation visibility, support route, qualified updates, consumer-visible recovery, and safe diagnostics matter without implying a control mechanism or service commitment.

## Earlier concerns confirmed and interpretations challenged

- Direct consumer testimony supports SES-005's producer/consumer responsibility and completion distinction (FND-029, FND-031) and SES-004's cross-boundary support and outcome concern (FND-004, STMT-098 through STMT-109). These remain preliminary because no source records were inspected.
- It limits an internal interpretation that centralized documentation or a sent specification is enough for understanding and readiness (HYP-003; STMT-119, STMT-161). It also challenges counting credential issuance, notice sent, or provider deployment as partner completion (STMT-121, STMT-122, STMT-160). It does not invalidate the underlying possible value of shared context.
- It challenges any assumption that Platform Support owns a partner symptom merely because it is reachable, while supporting its coordination role (STMT-098, STMT-116, STMT-174).

## Tensions

Self-service versus human interpretation; security safeguards versus speed; stable meaning versus producer delivery speed; central documentation versus domain semantics; provider completion versus partner readiness; platform coordination versus domain and security authority; traffic protection versus predictability; and operational transparency versus sensitive-data minimization. Borrowed test access is locally efficient but shifts attribution, scope, cleanup, and support risk. Two estimates in exchanges 7 and 18 are explicitly unverified.

## Discovery-method decisions and explicit non-decisions

Separate Northstar-controlled time from partner approvals and waiting; compare document tasks rather than page existence; test successful intended calls rather than issuance records; trace notice, comprehension, validation, adoption, and residual use independently; compare consumer-reported symptoms with safe platform/domain evidence. Consumer preferences are not contract terms. No requirement, authentication or credential mechanism, quota/rate-limit mechanism, versioning rule, compatibility policy, support commitment, SLA/SLO, technology, architecture baseline, ADR, or implementation is approved.
