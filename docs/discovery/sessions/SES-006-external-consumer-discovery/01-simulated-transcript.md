# SES-006 - External Consumer Discovery: Simulated Transcript

Fictional, simulated dialogue for a professional portfolio. All people, organizations, situations, estimates, and statements below are invented; no live interview, contract, incident, or system record was examined. Numbered exchanges preserve participant disagreement. Analytical IDs are intentionally absent from dialogue.

## 1. First-time partner onboarding

1. **Daniel Okafor:** Rachel, start before an access request. How would your team find the API and decide whether its business use fits? Which internal assumptions about partners would you correct?
2. **Rachel Kim:** A catalog entry could tell us an API exists yet leave the eligibility rules, data meaning, and supported use case unclear. We ask a person before committing engineering time. A self-service route is welcome for routine facts; a named human is still needed for ambiguous fit. That preference is not a contractual entitlement.
3. **Sofia Novak:** We assumed centralized documentation and visible onboarding status might reduce contacts. Does a document link plus an issued credential count as ready from your side?
4. **Rachel Kim:** No. Availability is not usability: I need a working example, error interpretation, and the meaning of a field in the commerce journey. Credential issuance is only an administrative event. Usable access means our authorized test can actually complete in the intended environment.
5. **Aisha Bello:** Commerce can explain business eligibility and order meaning; Platform can coordinate shared access. A single centrally maintained page could lag a domain interpretation. We need to know who can correct each part without sending the partner in circles.
6. **Jordan Lee:** In a first-time path I would distinguish organization and contact context, review prerequisites, test environment availability, access activation, and a support route. I cannot claim each stage currently has a reliable owner or timestamp.
7. **Rachel Kim:** One integration I recall needed roughly two to four weeks from first request to a verified test call. That estimate is explicitly unverified, includes our own approvals and waiting, and cannot be treated as a platform delay or target. A runnable example could reduce guesswork, but it cannot resolve a business rule.
8. **Elena García:** Speed does not remove identity, purpose, scope, or safe handling questions. Which prerequisites were understandable, and where did a review feel like repeated submission?
9. **Rachel Kim:** We can provide purpose and contacts once, if the requested evidence and owner are visible. In the imagined case, separate forms asked for similar facts with different labels. Our procurement and release approvals are ours; Northstar should not count them as its review queue.
10. **Daniel Okafor:** What would you call production-ready and launched? **Rachel Kim:** Our test must exercise the intended semantics, support routing must be known, and our release owner must accept the schedule. A provider deployment and credential email do not prove consumer readiness or first supported production use.

## 2. Credential and access problem

11. **Daniel Okafor:** Now assume a credential is issued but the intended test call fails. What happens before anyone knows the cause?
12. **Rachel Kim:** We may see an authorization-looking error while not knowing whether the contact, purpose, environment, scope, account state, or endpoint is wrong. I want a case owner and a safe way to share a timestamp, request identifier, environment, and symptom; I should not paste secrets or full customer payloads into chat.
13. **Jordan Lee:** Platform Support can coordinate the case and check shared context, but it may not authorize access or decide Commerce eligibility. The reachable desk is not necessarily the decision owner.
14. **Elena García:** Security may need to interpret an access exception or verify purpose, but a failed call is not proof of a security-review defect. We need the authorized decision path and bounded diagnostic evidence, including removal and closure if temporary access is used.
15. **Rachel Kim:** The locally quick workaround is borrowing an already working colleague's test access. It moves risk to attribution, scope, later cleanup, and our support team. I would rather wait for a clear route than make that workaround the normal path.
16. **Sofia Novak:** My team can track partner waiting and explain the next handoff; it cannot issue credentials or promise approval. A status saying “sent to Security” without an accepted owner gives us little to tell Rachel.
17. **Aisha Bello:** If the rejection is really a Commerce eligibility rule, we owe the interpretation. If it is shared access configuration, Platform may diagnose. Security speed and protection matter together; a universal shortcut would hide the distinction.
18. **Rachel Kim:** I recall perhaps three or four handoffs in one access issue, but that count is explicitly unverified and may include our own escalation. The elapsed time should separate active investigation, our waiting, and returned requests.

## 3. Breaking or semantic change

19. **Daniel Okafor:** Suppose the producer deploys a change and believes it compatible. What notice and proof would you need?
20. **Rachel Kim:** A syntactically unchanged field can carry different business meaning. We need a clear description of affected use cases, examples, expected behavior, and a contact who owns the semantics. Publishing a page or deployment note alone does not tell us whether our integration is safe.
21. **Aisha Bello:** Commerce owns the intended business meaning and should explain it. Our delivery date cannot establish Meridian's release capacity; we would need to understand impact and the partner's planned validation before describing completion.
22. **Rachel Kim:** Our release calendar is independent. Advance notice is a strong preference, while any contractual notice period would have to come from an actual agreement, which nobody has reviewed here. A version label alone does not guarantee compatibility.
23. **Jordan Lee:** A shared change record could make notice and active state visible, but distribution, revision history, and versioning mechanism remain open. We should compare what the partner saw with what the producer intended, including partial activation or rollback.
24. **Sofia Novak:** We sometimes treat “notice sent” as a handoff complete. Rachel's account challenges that: receipt, comprehension, test opportunity, and adoption can be separate. Partner coordination can relay status but cannot approve a semantic change.
25. **Elena García:** If a change affects authorization or data handling, Security needs an applicable review, yet that does not make Security the owner of business compatibility. A notice should avoid sensitive partner details and have an authorized audience.
26. **Rachel Kim:** Migration is complete for us when our active integration passes the agreed business checks and old behavior is no longer needed or residual use is explicitly accepted. Provider deployment can precede that by a release cycle or more; that duration is a possibility, not a verified estimate or commitment.
27. **Daniel Okafor:** Earlier internal work separated producer publication from consumer adoption. Does this support it, and what is overstated? **Rachel Kim:** It supports the separation. The internal idea that a centralized page or publication event can make us ready is overstated. The owner of meaning and an independently scheduled consumer check matter.

## 4. Traffic and support incident

28. **Daniel Okafor:** In a launch or incident, what traffic information and support communication would be useful before choosing any control mechanism?
29. **Rachel Kim:** We need to know expected usage shape, what symptoms indicate a constraint, and where to ask about a planned spike. Visibility helps us plan; it does not mean we request a particular quota or rate-limit design. Unexplained rejection feels like instability from our side.
30. **Jordan Lee:** A sudden surge could be partner retry behavior, domain degradation, or shared runtime behavior. Platform may see request and configuration signals, but we must not infer cause or consumer outcome from traffic graphs alone.
31. **Aisha Bello:** Commerce can interpret whether the transaction outcome is correct. It cannot by itself diagnose shared runtime behavior. We need a route that preserves domain authority while responders coordinate quickly.
32. **Rachel Kim:** During a consumer-visible failure I want an acknowledgement route, scope of known impact, next update path, and a way to correct an inaccurate status. I prefer predictable updates, but no SLA, SLO, frequency, or support commitment is established by this interview.
33. **Sofia Novak:** Partner coordination can communicate verified status and collect symptoms. It cannot accept Commerce risk, approve a production change, or guarantee restoration. When ownership is unclear, silence makes the external wait worse.
34. **Elena García:** Share only diagnostics necessary for the purpose: time window, correlation reference where available, environment, observed status, and sanitized example. Operational transparency must be balanced with minimization; raw credentials and broad payload capture are not justified.
35. **Rachel Kim:** Closure needs our observed recovery and a support route for recurrence, not just a green infrastructure dashboard. Our own retries may have amplified the issue, so we should test that claim against logs before assigning blame. The internally assumed platform ownership of every partner-facing symptom would be wrong.
36. **Daniel Okafor:** Which architecture questions have stronger evidence, and what sources do we each owe? **Rachel Kim:** Partner-facing metadata authority, change notice and adoption state, support routing, and safe outcome evidence now have a consumer account, but no design is settled. I will seek sanitized onboarding, failed-call, change, release, and incident examples plus any applicable agreement. **Sofia Novak:** I will map accepted support handoffs and partner communications. **Elena García:** I will identify access and disclosure authority sources. **Aisha Bello:** I will provide Commerce meaning and change samples. **Jordan Lee:** I will seek activation, correlation, and traffic-context samples. **Daniel Okafor:** I will reconcile these with earlier records and keep decisions open.
