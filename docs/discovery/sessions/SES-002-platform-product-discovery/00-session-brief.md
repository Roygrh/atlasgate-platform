# SES-002 - Platform Product Discovery: Session Brief

> **Fictional-simulation disclaimer:** Northstar Digital Group, this session, every participant, example, and claim are fictional simulations created for a professional portfolio. This is not a real meeting or operational record.

- **Session ID:** SES-002
- **Lifecycle state at creation:** Prepared for simulation
- **Purpose:** Explore prospective API-platform consumers, materially different current delivery and onboarding journeys, friction, workarounds, adoption barriers, alternatives, evidence gaps, and outcome-oriented candidate measures.
- **Facilitator:** Daniel Okafor. He is responsible for neutral prompts, balanced participation, separating reports from analysis, challenging universal-workflow assumptions, labeling estimates, preserving disagreement, and assigning evidence requests.

## Objectives and agenda

Objectives are to identify preliminary segments; trace distinct current journeys; expose handoffs, queues, rework, unclear ownership, missing information, and dependencies; compare workarounds and adoption incentives; challenge weak measures; and assign evidence requests. The 75-minute agenda is framing (5 minutes), actors and segments (10), three journey walkthroughs (35), alternatives and adoption (10), measures (10), and evidence commitments (5).

## Preparation

Participants review the [company profile](../../01-company-profile.md), [stakeholder map](../../02-stakeholder-map.md), [SES-001 record](../SES-001-executive-kickoff/02-session-record.md), and [assumptions and questions](../../04-assumptions-and-open-questions.md). They bring fictional recollections of one representative journey; no recollection is treated as verified source data.

## Planned questions and required examples

- Who produces, consumes, reviews, supports, and operates APIs, and where do those roles overlap?
- Which consumer groups have materially different jobs, constraints, and alternatives?
- How does work move from idea to usable operation, and where do handoffs, queues, rework, dependencies, or missing inputs appear?
- Which local workaround is faster, and what downstream cost or risk does it create?
- What makes a shared path attractive or avoidable without assuming mandatory migration?
- Which measures demonstrate completed outcomes rather than API counts or portal logins?
- Required examples: a commerce API or integration for an external partner launch; a loyalty API change consumed by internal product teams; and external-partner onboarding to discover, access, test, and support an API.

## Evidence rules

Dialogue contains no analytical IDs. Claims remain attributed; estimates are explicitly unverified; conflicting accounts remain visible. Derived statements, segments, journeys, findings, hypotheses, risks, and measures must link back to transcript sections. No single session validates enterprise prevalence.

## Non-goals

The session does not approve requirements, prioritize a roadmap, select technology, accept architecture, mandate migration, define implementation scope, set targets, establish cost-benefit claims, or authorize delivery.

## Exit criteria

All six perspectives appear; the three journeys remain distinct and preliminary; at least four substantive tensions and one locally faster but costly workaround are captured; producer, consumer, reviewer, operator, support, and partner needs are distinguished; raw API-count and portal-login metrics are challenged; and evidence requests end with fictional owners rather than consensus.
