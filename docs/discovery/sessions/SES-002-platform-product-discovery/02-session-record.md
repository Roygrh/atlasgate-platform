# SES-002 - Platform Product Discovery: Session Record

> **Fictional-simulation disclaimer:** This record analyzes a fictional simulated session for the AtlasGate professional portfolio. It is not evidence of a real meeting, organization, process, or performance result.

## Summary

SES-002 explored producers, consumers, reviewers, operators, support roles, and partner needs across three distinct current-state journeys. Participants reported repeated handoffs, incomplete inputs, informal coordination, uncertain ownership, and external dependencies, while challenging a universal workflow and output-only adoption measures. All claims remain preliminary.

## Themes and tensions

- Common governance evidence may help, but one common sequence would obscure different triggers, audiences, authority, and dependencies.
- Faster local workarounds can reduce immediate delay while weakening attribution, cleanup, ownership, support, or visibility.
- Discoverability helps, while internal and external audiences require different information and eligibility treatment.
- Review queues are visible, but missing inputs and external waiting may be wrongly attributed to platform or domain teams.
- Voluntary adoption may signal usability, but API counts and portal logins do not demonstrate completed outcomes.
- Consistent information competes with dated-launch transition cost; no mandatory migration was assumed.

## Discovery-process decisions

The group agreed only to preserve three separate journey drafts, use preliminary segments, label waiting causes where evidence permits, treat measures as candidates, and collect contrasting samples. These are discovery-method choices, not product or architecture decisions.

## Explicit non-decisions

No requirement, roadmap priority, capability, workflow, target, technology, vendor, architecture, migration, implementation scope, control, service commitment, or investment case was approved.

## Evidence limitations

The session is a simulation based on six perspectives and recalled examples. No tickets, timelines, messages, access records, partner feedback, inventory, telemetry, incident data, obligation source, or cost record was examined. The six-to-eight-week and three-engineer-day estimates are explicitly unverified. Partner developers, security, compliance, SRE, procurement, and consumer product teams did not participate directly.
