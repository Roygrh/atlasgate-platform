# Platform Product Discovery Synthesis

This synthesis derives from the fictional simulated [SES-002 record](sessions/SES-002-platform-product-discovery/02-session-record.md). It is preliminary evidence, not approved scope, requirements, roadmap, architecture, target, or investment proof.

## Consumer landscape

The preliminary [consumer segments](consumers/consumer-segments.md) distinguish domain producers, internal consumers, external partner developers, security or compliance reviewers, and platform operator/support teams. A role may overlap with another, but their jobs and information boundaries differ. Reviewer and partner-developer needs require direct follow-up.

## Journey summaries and differences

The [current-state journeys](journeys/current-api-delivery-journeys.md) remain distinct. A commerce partner launch begins with a commercial date and crosses an external readiness boundary. A loyalty change depends on consumer discovery, semantics, compatibility, and independent internal release trains. Partner onboarding begins with eligibility and discovery, then access, testing, production transition, and support. Shared handoff and information themes do not establish one universal workflow.

## Common friction and journey-specific differences

Reported common friction includes incomplete context, returned inputs, manual handoffs, informal ownership lookup, document drift, repeated interpretation, external waiting, and limited dependency visibility. Commerce includes launch urgency and partner readiness; loyalty includes unknown consumers and release cadence; onboarding includes eligibility, identity, procurement, audience separation, and support context. Stage attribution is missing across all three.

## Workarounds and alternatives

Alternatives include email documents, chat and direct contacts, local trackers, internal directories, copied configuration, expert memory, partner-specific calls, and temporary shared test credentials. Some are faster locally. The reported downstream costs include weak attribution, drift, cleanup, hidden dependencies, version divergence, support load, and understated demand. Prevalence and realized harm remain unknown.

## Adoption incentives and barriers

Potential incentives are fewer repeated fields, reusable evidence, visible ownership, faster completed outcomes, clearer status, and actionable support. Barriers include added intake, central queues, duplicated review, transition work during dated launches, irrelevant audience steps, unclear eligibility, and mandatory migration. Voluntary selection is only a candidate signal and must be paired with outcomes.

## Product hypotheses

The [hypothesis register](hypotheses/product-hypotheses.md) proposes tests around reusable journey context, internal change information, partner discoverability/status, and voluntary shared paths. Each proposition includes limiting evidence and a rejection signal; none is supported or approved.

## Candidate and misleading measures

Candidate measures are end-to-end elapsed time by labeled stage; queue, rework, and missing-input time; manual handoffs; active time to first successful call; support contacts; change incidents; completed journey outcomes; and the proportion of eligible journeys voluntarily choosing a shared path. Baselines, targets, data availability, pause rules, and attribution remain open. API counts, raw portal logins, and adoption alone are misleading because they can reward fragmentation, misread low-frequency success, or measure use without value.

## Explicit non-decisions and remaining evidence

SES-002 did not approve requirements, capabilities, scope, a roadmap, workflow, migration, measures, targets, technology, architecture, or funding. Needed evidence includes contrasting journey records, direct internal and external consumer accounts, partner categories, ticket and support samples, inventory and ownership sources, review and obligation evidence, stage timestamps, pause attribution, workaround prevalence, incidents, costs, and authority.

## Implications for future discovery

Future discovery should validate segments and journeys separately before comparing common evidence needs. The next planned security and compliance activity should examine obligations by flow, review timing, exceptions, evidence, assets, threats, and authority. Later product synthesis can test whether common governance supports variants without turning similarity into forced standardization.
