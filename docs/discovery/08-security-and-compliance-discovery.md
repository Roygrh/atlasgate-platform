# Security and Compliance Discovery Synthesis

This synthesis derives from the fictional simulated [SES-003 record](sessions/SES-003-security-and-compliance-discovery/02-session-record.md). It is preliminary discovery analysis, not legal advice, a compliance claim, an approved requirement, a completed threat model, selected control, target architecture, or implementation evidence.

## Assets, boundaries, and scenarios

The [security context](security/security-context.md) identifies preliminary customer/order/transaction information, loyalty identifiers/balances/eligibility, identity context, credentials/access configuration, decision records, and diagnostic telemetry. Candidate boundaries span partner/Northstar, test/production, domain/platform-support, human/service, producer/consumer, and telemetry/audit-evidence contexts. No classification or boundary is validated.

Commerce partner access raises external organization, service identity, production ownership, and mixed-wait questions. Loyalty change raises downstream purpose, service actor, cloned access, and consumer-audience questions. Urgent exceptions raise authority, missing evidence, time bounds, monitoring, reassessment, removal, and closure. Production support raises tension between rapid diagnosis and attributable, necessary, purpose- and time-bounded cross-boundary access.

## Obligations, threats, and candidate objectives

The [obligation candidates](security/obligation-candidates.md) preserve possible policy, contractual, privacy, market-rule, governance, incident, and records-source questions without asserting applicability. The [threat scenarios](security/threat-scenarios.md) frame retained partner access, obsolete cloned access, indefinite exceptions, shared-credential attribution loss, broad support access, and excessive evidence duplication.

Candidate objectives are outcome-oriented and unapproved: preserve attribution; bind access to current purpose, owner, environment, and duration; enable review and removal; make deviations accountable, monitored, expiring, reassessed, and verifiably closed; minimize evidence while preserving sufficient integrity and context. No mechanism, product, or centralized enforcement model is selected.

## Access lifecycle, timing, and exceptions

Access questions span request, ownership, assessment, approval, issuance, use, review, change, suspension, removal, and closure. Existing coverage is unknown. Review timing must distinguish queue, active assessment, missing inputs, redesign rework, and external waiting. Two recalled estimates—four or five business days of mixed review/contact time and roughly half a day to assemble an exception package—are unverified.

The [exception cases](security/exception-cases.md) remain preliminary or review planned. They preserve requested deviation, business driver, assets, missing evidence, authority, candidate conditions, expiry, extension, cleanup, and closure. Security advice is not assumed to equal business-risk acceptance or formal approval authority.

## Evidence, telemetry, and minimization

The [evidence requirements](security/evidence-requirements.md) distinguish decision/audit evidence from operational telemetry. Approval, access, exception, expiry, and closure metadata may support a review purpose. Logs, traces, metrics, and request identifiers primarily support diagnosis unless validated for another purpose. Integrity, provenance, access, context, and lifecycle may differ.

Evidence requests favor metadata, redacted samples, aggregates, and minimum necessary content. Full payloads or log bodies are not assumed necessary. No retention period, legal applicability, evidence sufficiency, or privacy conclusion is established.

## Non-decisions and remaining evidence

No universal classification, checklist, identity model, approval route, authority, retention period, obligation, requirement, control objective, control, product, technology, architecture, exception, or ADR was approved. Needed evidence includes authoritative source categories and interpreters; sanitized review, access, exception, closure, partner, and support samples; reason-coded timing; ownership sources; telemetry inventory; consumer evidence; and a decision-authority map.
